## Non-mutating inspection strategy

1. In the current saved document, read each component/body's name, parent component, solid count, volume, bounding box, and centroid. Compare the Z screw against the cylinder contract above. Compare the yoke against the offline `CBP-Z303_BED_FORK_YOKE` counterpart and angle against its expected one-solid volume **3942.553554 mm³** / bounds `(-9,-16,183.5)` to `(11,38,203.5)`. The offline yoke's saved one-solid volume is **71072.590406 mm³**; native fillet/kernel differences require a stated tolerance rather than silent acceptance.
2. Read the listed feature objects' `name`, `operation`, `parentComponent`, `healthState`, `errorOrWarningMessage`, `bodies`, and `linkedFeatures`. For every returned body, record its actual parent component. A foreign component among a feature's modified/created bodies is direct evidence of escaped scope. Missing historical result bodies must be recorded as unavailable, not treated as a pass.
3. Do **not** set participants, change operation, hide bodies, suppress/delete features, rebuild03/07, call `rollTo`, or move the timeline during this first inspection. Autodesk documents that accessing/editing existing `participantBodies` requires the timeline immediately before that feature. Therefore a strictly non-mutating end-of-timeline inspection should use current `feature.bodies` and geometry; defer historical participant confirmation to a separate checkpointed diagnostic copy if needed.
4. Only after the inspection evidence is saved should a separate repair plan be prepared. A clean health-state/sketch-constraint audit cannot establish correct cut ownership or restore missing material.
